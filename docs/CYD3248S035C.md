# CYD 3.5-inch ESP32-3248S035C, 480 × 320 (experimental)

This is the 3.5-inch "CYD" sold under the Sunton name as the **ESP32-3248S035C**: a 320 x 480 panel with an
**ST7796** driver over SPI and **GT911 capacitive touch** over I2C, on an ESP32 with 4 MB flash and no PSRAM. It is
the same family as the CYD ESP32-2432S028 and the Hosyond 4-inch: the same chip and the same kind of display bus, but
with capacitive glass, so there is no touch calibration.

The board is **experimental**: its file was built from a working ESPHome configuration of the board, and it builds
with the others, but the project has not tried the ESP Screens firmware on a real unit yet.

Take the **C** variant (capacitive). The ESP32-3248S035R is the same board with resistive XPT2046 touch, which this
board file does not drive.

## Install

Update ESP Screen Manager and choose **CYD · 3.5 inch** (ESP32-3248S035C) in **New screen**. Follow
[Easy setup](EASY_SETUP.md) to create a profile with its own name, Wi-Fi references and unique API/OTA keys, then
flash it over USB or download the firmware for ESPHome Web.

The remote package is `packages/cyd3248s035c.yaml`; the checkout entry is `checkout/cyd3248s035c.yaml`. Both combine
`packages/core.yaml` with `packages/boards/cyd3248s035c.yaml`.

## Layout and capabilities

- Lying down: 480 x 320, two columns of three cells, as on the Hosyond 4-inch. Standing up: 320 x 480, one column of
  four.
- The compact look, drawn at 140 dpi. The glass has 165 dpi (577 diagonal pixels over 3.5 inches), and at that
  density the look's fonts would push the firmware over the flash budget of a 4 MB board, so letters and keys are
  about 15 % smaller in millimetres than on the CYD and the Hosyond. Even at 140 dpi the firmware takes 96.9 % of the
  update slot on the oldest ESPHome the packages allow (2026.6.2), close to the 97 % limit, so this board has little
  room for Override YAML.
- 4 MB of flash with the wide partition table of [UPDATING_4MB_SCREENS.md](UPDATING_4MB_SCREENS.md): a new screen is
  flashed with it over USB, so there is nothing to move.
- Capacitive touch: pages change with a swipe inward from the left or right edge, as on the other capacitive boards.
- The backlight is a PWM pin (GPIO27): dimming, standby, night and an alert's flashes work as on the CYD.
- The RGB LED on the board is the **LED** light in Home Assistant.
- No camera pictures and no media artwork, as on the CYD: there is no PSRAM.
- LVGL draws through an 8 % buffer (24 KB), as on the Hosyond 4-inch, since everything shares the chip's own memory.
  Read `sensor.<screen>_heap_free` with a full layout; under 40 KB is too little.

## Pins

| Part | Pins |
| --- | --- |
| SPI bus (display) | CLK GPIO14, MOSI GPIO13, MISO GPIO12 |
| Display (ST7796) | CS GPIO15, DC GPIO2 |
| I2C bus (touch) | SDA GPIO33, SCL GPIO32 |
| Touch (GT911) | reset GPIO25; its interrupt (GPIO21) stays unused, the panel is polled |
| Backlight | GPIO27, lit at a high level |
| RGB LED | red GPIO22, green GPIO17, blue GPIO16, lit at a low level |

## Still to check on a real unit

- The colours (`DISPLAY_INVERT_COLORS`) and the mirroring of the picture.
- Which way the touch axes run. The board takes none of them swapped or mirrored, reading the GT911 in the panel's
  own pixels; a screen whose taps land mirrored can set `TOUCH_MIRROR_X` or `TOUCH_MIRROR_Y` to `"true"` in its own
  YAML ([Hardware-specific YAML overrides](EASY_SETUP.md)).
- The colours of the RGB LED.
- The free memory with a full layout, and whether the draw buffer can go to 12 %.
- The share of the 4 MB firmware slot the build takes.
