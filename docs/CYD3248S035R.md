# CYD 3.5-inch ESP32-3248S035R, 480 × 320 (experimental)

This is the 3.5-inch "CYD" sold under the Sunton name as the **ESP32-3248S035R**: a 320 x 480 panel with an
**ST7796** driver and **XPT2046 resistive touch** on one SPI bus, on an ESP32 with 4 MB flash and no PSRAM. It is the
resistive twin of the [ESP32-3248S035C](CYD3248S035C.md): the same glass, drawn the same way, with the touch of the
2.8-inch CYD and the Hosyond 4-inch.

The board is **experimental**: its file was built from a working ESPHome configuration of the board, and it builds
with the others, but the project has not tried the ESP Screens firmware on a real unit yet.

## Install

Update ESP Screen Manager and choose **CYD · 3.5 inch** (ESP32-3248S035R) in **New screen**. Follow
[Easy setup](EASY_SETUP.md) to create a profile with its own name, Wi-Fi references and unique API/OTA keys, then
flash it over USB or download the firmware for ESPHome Web.

The remote package is `packages/cyd3248s035r.yaml`; the checkout entry is `checkout/cyd3248s035r.yaml`. Both combine
`packages/core.yaml` with `packages/boards/cyd3248s035r.yaml`, which shares its display with the capacitive board
(`packages/hardware/cyd-3248s035.yaml`) and its touch controller with the Hosyond 4-inch
(`packages/hardware/xpt2046-spi.yaml`).

## Touch calibration

The first start shows the calibration: tap each crosshair as on the 2.8-inch CYD ([CALIBRATING.md](CALIBRATING.md)).
The correction is kept on the screen and survives updates; **Calibrate touch** in Home Assistant measures again.
Before that, the board reads the panel with the raw range the working configuration measured (x 282 to 3835 and
y 208 to 3823, in the panel's own axes) and both axes mirrored.

## Layout and capabilities

- Lying down: 480 x 320, two columns of three cells, as on the Hosyond 4-inch. Standing up: 320 x 480, one column of
  four.
- The compact look, drawn at 140 dpi as on the ESP32-3248S035C: the glass has 165 dpi, and the look's fonts at that
  density do not fit a 4 MB board's firmware slot, so letters and keys are about 15 % smaller in millimetres than on
  the 2.8-inch CYD and the Hosyond.
- 4 MB of flash with the wide partition table of [UPDATING_4MB_SCREENS.md](UPDATING_4MB_SCREENS.md): a new screen is
  flashed with it over USB, so there is nothing to move.
- Resistive touch: pages change with a quick swipe across the tiles, and a swipe from the top or bottom edge opens the
  settings page or goes home, as on the 2.8-inch CYD.
- The backlight is a PWM pin (GPIO27): dimming, standby, night and an alert's flashes work as on the CYD.
- The RGB LED on the board is the **LED** light in Home Assistant.
- No camera pictures and no media artwork, as on the CYD: there is no PSRAM.
- LVGL draws through an 8 % buffer (24 KB), as on the Hosyond 4-inch, since everything shares the chip's own memory.
  Read `sensor.<screen>_heap_free` with a full layout; under 40 KB is too little.
- The light sensor on the board (GPIO34) is not read.

## Pins

| Part | Pins |
| --- | --- |
| SPI bus (display and touch) | CLK GPIO14, MOSI GPIO13, MISO GPIO12 |
| Display (ST7796) | CS GPIO15, DC GPIO2 |
| Touch (XPT2046) | CS GPIO33; its interrupt (GPIO36) stays unused, as on the CYD |
| Backlight | GPIO27, lit at a high level |
| RGB LED | red GPIO4, green GPIO17, blue GPIO16, lit at a low level |

## Still to check on a real unit

- The colours (`DISPLAY_INVERT_COLORS`) and the mirroring of the picture.
- Which way the touch axes run before a calibration. The board takes both mirrored and none swapped, worked out from
  the working configuration; a screen whose crosshair taps land far off can set `TOUCH_MIRROR_X` or `TOUCH_MIRROR_Y`
  in its own YAML ([Hardware-specific YAML overrides](EASY_SETUP.md)).
- The touch threshold. The board takes the CYD's 800, so a light brush of the film is not a touch; the working
  configuration ran 40. A panel that misses light taps can take a lower `threshold` in its own YAML.
- The display's SPI clock. The board runs it at 40 MHz as the Hosyond does; the working configuration ran 80 MHz.
- The colours of the RGB LED.
- The free memory with a full layout, and whether the draw buffer can go to 12 %.
